## The Universal Research Framework

#### A Framework for Organizing Interdisciplinary Scientific Research

> **4.1 Why Do We Need Another Research Framework?**

#### Science Has Become Increasingly Fragmented

The remarkable success of modern science has been built upon
specialization. Scientific disciplines have developed increasingly
sophisticated theories, experimental methods, computational approaches,
and professional communities. This specialization has enabled
extraordinary advances across physics, chemistry, biology, medicine,
engineering, artificial intelligence, economics, psychology, and many
other fields.

However, specialization has also created new challenges.

Many of the most important scientific questions no longer belong to a
single discipline.

Artificial Intelligence combines computer science, mathematics,
neuroscience, psychology, linguistics, and philosophy.

Systems Biology integrates molecular biology, mathematics, computer
science, physics, and engineering.

Climate science requires atmospheric physics, ecology, chemistry,
economics, and public policy.

Precision agriculture combines biology, remote sensing, artificial
intelligence, environmental science, and engineering.

Scientific discovery itself increasingly depends upon interdisciplinary
collaboration.

As disciplinary boundaries become more permeable, organizing
interdisciplinary research has become one of the central methodological
challenges of contemporary science.

This chapter proposes that interdisciplinary research should be
organized not only according to **disciplines**, but also according to
**universal scientific principles** that recur across multiple domains.  **The Problem**

Current research organization is largely administrative.

Universities are organized into departments.

Funding agencies are organized into subject areas.

Scientific journals represent disciplines.

Professional societies represent specialties.

While this organization has served science well, it is not always
optimal for investigating problems that naturally span multiple fields.

A researcher interested in intelligence may simultaneously require
knowledge from

-   biology,

-   neuroscience,

-   psychology,

-   artificial intelligence,

-   linguistics,

-   education,

-   philosophy.

-   Policy and governance

The scientific question does not belong exclusively to any one
discipline.

The organization of science therefore becomes increasingly different
from the organization of knowledge.

#### A Different Perspective

Instead of beginning with disciplines,

begin with questions.

Instead of asking

> **Which department owns this research?**

ask

> **Which universal scientific principles are involved?**

This seemingly simple shift changes the organization of research.

#### The Philosophy

The Universal Research Framework rests upon one central idea.

> **Scientific disciplines study different phenomena. Universal principles study recurring patterns across those phenomena.**

The objective is therefore not to replace disciplines.

The objective is to connect them.

#### Why This Matters

This framework changes the unit of scientific organization.

Traditional organization begins with

Departments.

The Universal Research Framework begins with

Questions.

Traditional organization classifies disciplines.

The Universal Research Framework classifies **relationships**.

Traditional organization separates knowledge.

The Universal Research Framework seeks to connect knowledge.

#### Design Principles

The framework has been designed to satisfy six methodological
principles.

#### Generality

Applicable across scientific disciplines.

#### Scalability

Applicable to individual papers, research programs, institutions, or
national initiatives.

#### Recursiveness

The same framework can organize research at multiple levels of scale.

#### Domain Independence

The framework is independent of any single scientific discipline.

#### Extensibility

New domains and new research programs can be incorporated without
changing the underlying architecture.

#### Testability

The usefulness of the framework should be evaluated by whether it
improves the organization, communication, planning, and integration of
interdisciplinary research.

#### Introduction

The Universal Research Framework proposes that interdisciplinary
scientific research can be organized through a set of recurring
conceptual perspectives referred to as **Universal Scientific
Frameworks**.

These frameworks are **not scientific disciplines**.

Nor are they research methods.

Instead, they represent fundamental ways of examining scientific
problems that recur across multiple domains of inquiry.

A biologist, economist, engineer, physicist, or AI researcher may all
investigate different phenomena while employing the same conceptual
framework.

For example, a systems perspective may be applied equally to ecosystems,
neural networks, supply chains, educational institutions, or artificial
intelligence.

Similarly, questions concerning information, organization, emergence, or
complexity appear across a wide range of disciplines.

The purpose of these frameworks is therefore to provide a common
conceptual language capable of supporting interdisciplinary dialogue.

> **Why These Eight Frameworks?**

Many important scientific concepts could have been selected.

Examples include

-   Evolution

-   Adaptation

-   Networks

-   Energy

-   Optimization

-   Robustness

-   Entropy

-   Computation

The present framework does not suggest that these concepts are
unimportant.

Rather, the eight selected frameworks were chosen because they satisfy
three criteria.

#### Criterion 1

They occur across a wide range of scientific disciplines.

#### Criterion 2

They help organize scientific thinking rather than describe a single
phenomenon.

#### Criterion 3

They naturally complement one another when investigating complex
systems.

The eight frameworks should therefore be regarded as a proposed starting
point for interdisciplinary organization rather than as a fixed or
exhaustive taxonomy.

The lower levels describe increasingly organized systems.

The higher levels describe increasingly sophisticated behaviors that
arise from those systems.

This progression provides the conceptual architecture for the Universal
Research Framework.

#### Framework I

#### Systems

#### Formal Definition

A **system** is a collection of interacting components whose collective
behaviour depends upon the relationships among those components as well
as their individual properties.

#### Central Scientific Question

How do interactions among components generate system-level behaviour?

> **Why Systems?**

Almost every scientific discipline studies systems.

Examples include

-   biological systems

-   ecological systems

-   nervous systems

-   economic systems

-   educational systems

-   artificial intelligence systems

The systems framework therefore provides one of the broadest
perspectives available for interdisciplinary research.

#### Applications

Biology

Cells, organisms, ecosystems.

Artificial Intelligence

Multi-agent systems.

Economics

Markets.

Education

Learning environments.

Medicine

Human physiology.

Agriculture

Cropping systems.

#### Framework II

#### Architecture

#### Formal Definition

**Architecture** describes the structural arrangement of components within a system that constrains interactions and enables function.  **Central Scientific Question**

How does structure influence function?

Architecture investigates

-   organization of biological networks

-   neural architectures

-   software architectures

-   institutional architectures

-   educational architectures

The architecture framework emphasizes design rather than behaviour.

#### Framework III

#### Organization

#### Formal Definition

**Organization** is the coordinated arrangement of structures, processes, and interactions that enables a system to perform coherent functions.  **Central Scientific Question**

How does coordinated organization generate functional capability?

Organization differs from architecture.

Architecture concerns structure.

Organization concerns coordinated function.

This distinction is fundamental.

A building may possess architecture.

A living cell possesses organization.

#### Framework IV

#### Information

#### Formal Definition

**Information** represents meaningful differences that can be generated, transmitted, transformed, stored, interpreted, or utilized by a system.  **Central Scientific Question**

How is information generated, represented, and transformed?

Information appears throughout science.

Genes encode information.

Brains process information.

AI manipulates information.

Markets exchange information.

Education transfers information.

Scientific discovery generates information.

#### Framework V

#### Intelligence

#### Formal Definition

**Intelligence** is the capacity of a system to acquire, integrate, interpret, and apply information in order to achieve adaptive goals under changing conditions.  **Central Scientific Question**

How do systems learn, adapt, reason, and solve problems?

This definition intentionally includes

-   biological intelligence

-   artificial intelligence

-   collective intelligence

-   organizational intelligence

rather than restricting intelligence to human cognition.

#### Framework VI

#### Discovery

#### Formal Definition

**Discovery** is the process through which systems generate knowledge that was previously unavailable or unrecognized.  **Central Scientific Question**

How does genuinely new knowledge emerge?

Discovery occurs at multiple scales.

Scientific discovery.

Biological discovery.

Machine discovery.

Collective discovery.

This framework distinguishes discovery from information processing.

Discovery creates new knowledge.

#### Framework VII

#### Emergence

#### Formal Definition

**Emergence** refers to the appearance of system-level properties that arise through interactions among components and cannot be completely understood by examining the components individually.  **Central Scientific Question**

How do novel properties arise from organized interactions?

Examples include

-   life

-   consciousness

-   ecosystems

-   collective intelligence

-   scientific communities

Emergence represents one of the defining characteristics of complex
systems.

#### Framework VIII

#### Complexity

#### Formal Definition

**Complexity** describes systems containing numerous interacting components whose collective behaviour is dynamic, adaptive, nonlinear, and often difficult to predict completely.  **Central Scientific Question**

How do highly interconnected adaptive systems behave?

Complexity integrates many of the preceding frameworks.

Complex systems exhibit

organization,

information,

emergence,

adaptation,

and intelligence.

#### Relationships Among the Eight Frameworks

Although each framework can be studied independently, the greatest
